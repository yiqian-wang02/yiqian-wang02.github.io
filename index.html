About Yiqian

I am a third-year PhD student in the Linguistics Department at Harvard, specializing in semantics and pragmatics in natural languages, while also intrigued by how meaning is structured in other communicative or computational systems, and by what follows from those differences in structure.

Sitting at the interface between human cognition and communication, natural languages have the potential to reveal fundamental mechanisms employed by our minds to encode and transmit information. With such aspiration, I am drawn to the study of natural languages.

One question follows naturally: why do sentences in natural language organize/compress information into different types of inferences? As a first step, my projects zoom into one type of inference that many linguists call "presupposition," which has a special logical and contextual profile. My current project aims to explore why certain types of words "trigger" them. My prior project explored how presuppositions interact with implicatures, in the case of disjunction.

Beyond natural language, I am broadly interested in the semantics of formal systems: what kinds of correspondence hold between them, how they are ordered by expressiveness, and along what dimensions they trade off. Relatedly, I am interested in exploring how to specify what an AI system should "believe", and how to ensure it maintains consistency across its outputs, especially when some beliefs should be treated as more foundational than others.

If any of this resonates with you, I would love to hear from you! You can reach me at yiqian_wang@g.harvard.edu.

Background: Before Harvard, I completed my undergraduate study at Tsinghua University (2020–2024) in Beijing, where I built a foundation in linguistics, philosophical logic, English literature, mathematics, and cognitive psychology. I am proud to have been part of the Tsinghua Logic community.
